# Evidence-driven narrative writing contract

Use this contract when generating or revising an `oxygen.story/3` Project Story, Chapter Story, or
Insight. It governs selection and writing; it never authorizes access beyond the approved reviewed
Evidence boundary.

## Governing rule

> Write the complete supported Story first. Select sparse Insights afterward.

Consider the complete approved reviewed history. Preserve chronology, context, attribution,
failures, uncertainty, causal restraint, Privacy, Evidence traceability, and non-fabrication.
Chapter brevity, Insight density, importance, and Highlight worthiness are never selection,
generation, revision, or validation objectives.

Retain every reviewed-Evidence-supported unit that materially explains background, causal or
temporal relationships, participant interaction, judgment, ordinary progress needed for later
understanding, failed attempts, corrections, validation, consequences, or current state. Omit only
duplicated meaning, routine status with no explanatory contribution, material outside the Chapter
arc, or content withheld by Privacy. Readability may reorganize retained context but may not delete
it.

Never invent dialogue, people, emotion, motivation, Evidence, causal certainty, consensus, or a
cleaner ending than the reviewed record supports.

## Story-first generation order

Use these conceptual passes inside Build Project Story; do not create new top-level workflow stages:

1. understand the complete approved project history;
2. determine coherent Chapter narrative arcs;
3. write the complete ordered Chapter and Project Story narrative;
4. verify continuity, chronology, attribution, Evidence, causal restraint, Privacy, and
   uncertainty;
5. group adjacent Chapters into precise one- or two-word Phases;
6. only after the complete Story is understood, identify independently warranted learning moments;
7. produce zero or more Insights.

Story inclusion is independent of Insight worthiness. Never preselect learning moments and build
the Story around them.

## Chapter

A Chapter boundary is owned by a complete coherent narrative arc. A transcript, meeting, file,
source document, event count, fixed time slice, Insight count, importance score, Highlight label,
or reusable lesson does not define the boundary.

A supported Chapter may retain background, ordinary setup or progress, transitions, relationships
and handoffs, attempts, failures, disagreements, corrections, decisions, validation,
consequences, and unresolved/current state. It does not universally require drama, tension, a
breakthrough, a decisive turn, a problem-action-result template, a reusable lesson, or a separately
typed final `current_state` Chapter.

Current state must remain honest. Use its own Chapter when it forms a coherent arc; otherwise make
it the supported ending of the final Chapter. Do not rewrite an incomplete investigation as a
successful conclusion.

### Chapter title and overview

Use a concrete supported title that helps a reader anticipate the Chapter's arc. It may name a
constraint, attempt, capability, correction, result, or open boundary. It need not prove tension or
outcome and must not manufacture drama.

The overview briefly previews the supported background, meaningful participant action or project
movement, and result or open boundary. Avoid navigation instructions and repeated boilerplate.
Bind every factual claim to Chapter Evidence. Concrete nouns, active verbs, specific constraints,
and varied sentence rhythm may improve readability; jokes, invented color, dialogue, emotion,
motive, metaphor, anthropomorphism, and unsupported causality remain prohibited.

### Story construction

Write enough coherent prose to reconstruct the supported arc without reopening Evidence merely to
recover the basic Story. Material elements may include:

- the supported starting position or context;
- purpose, constraints, and alternatives;
- ordinary setup or progress that later reasoning depends on;
- attempts, failures, corrections, and rejected approaches;
- participant actions and responses;
- Evidence that changed understanding or direction;
- decisions and supported rationale;
- implementation, validation, consequence, or unresolved boundary.

These are coverage cues, not mandatory roles. Do not fabricate a missing universal problem, final
action, result, disagreement, lesson, or second actor. Do not split one connected arc to satisfy a
length target or combine unrelated arcs to reduce navigation count.

Chapter length follows the Evidence needed to reconstruct the arc. Do not impose a global word,
sentence, paragraph, or character target. Remove audit-report voice, command-by-command narration,
mechanical field enumeration, generic summary filler, and repeated status noise only when those
elements add no explanatory meaning.

### People and attribution

Every Chapter requires at least one Evidence-supported Person or actor. Humans, users, Agents,
reviewers, speakers, owners, and operators may be actors when supported. Use safe functional roles
and exact reviewed Evidence. Preserve role uncertainty; do not infer a name, employer, title,
identity, relationship, reply, or consensus.

Attribute supported actions, questions, corrections, reviews, approvals, and decisions to the
supported actor. When Evidence contains proposal -> objection -> revision -> agreement, preserve
that sequence. When it does not, omit the missing turn. Avoid generic `the team` wording when a
supported role is available.

### Evidence-bound relational writing

Determine the actual semantic relationship first and express it with the most natural construction.
A connective adverb is optional. Sequence, cause, response, contrast, correction, Evidence,
limitation, and continuation wording must not claim a stronger relationship than Evidence
supports.

- Use chronology language only for supported order.
- Use causal language only when Evidence establishes causality; temporal order is not causality.
- Use response or correction language only when one action actually answers or changes another.
- Use disagreement language only for real disagreement.
- State unresolved causes and limitations directly.

The vocabulary is open, not an allowlist. Prefer syntactic clarity over mechanical connector
counts or thesaurus substitution. Repeated sentence openings are an editorial concern, not a
brittle readiness rule.

## Project Story and Phase

The Project Story is the complete historical narrative within the approved reviewed boundary. Its
summary normally uses two or three concise sentences to establish the supported beginning, major
development, and ending boundary without mechanically listing every Chapter or metric.

After Chapter arcs and their order are complete, group adjacent Chapters into Phases. A Phase names
a coherent project period or state for homepage and Timeline presentation. It is not a review unit
and must not dictate, split, or merge Chapter boundaries.

Use precise one- or two-word labels. Do not force a Phase count or use generic fallbacks such as
`Project Evolution`, `General Work`, `Other`, or `Later Stage`. One Phase is valid when the ordered
Chapters genuinely share the same coherent period or state.

## Sparse Insight generation

Generate zero or more Insights (`0..n`) per Chapter only after the complete Story has been written
and verified. There is no minimum, maximum, quota, density target, or one-per-Chapter/per-block
rule. Multiple Insights are valid only when each is an independently warranted learning or
judgment moment. Zero Insights is a valid first-class result.

Every generated Insight contains exactly these four semantic meanings:

### Background

State the minimum Story-grounded context needed to understand the judgment moment. Do not rewrite
the Chapter or add general model knowledge.

### Quote

Ground the Insight through one or more exact safe reviewed Story-block anchors. The public Quote is
the safe Story moment or another post-policy-safe representation; never copy raw/private Evidence
or removed source language into the Insight.

### Directly Acquired Experience

State what was learned from that actual project moment, bounded by what was known then. Keep it
grounded in the Story and Evidence. Do not silently introduce general industry advice or hindsight
certainty.

### Principle

State a reusable rule, question, or guardrail for a genuinely similar future condition. Preserve
the trigger and bounded reason. Reject generic slogans and unsupported industry prior.

An Insight title is optional presentation metadata for navigation, scanability, localization, or
editing. It is not a fifth semantic meaning, and missing or weak title text alone does not determine
semantic validity.

Every Insight traces through the existing chain:

```text
Insight -> safe reviewed Story anchor(s) -> internal Evidence support
```

Keep factual history in Story. Label Insight interpretation as interpretation. AI-generated
Insights eventually require explicit human review of the currently presented version; silence is
not approval. Generation must not fabricate an Insight merely to create a review task.

## Passage assistance

No Story block is required to contain why-it-mattered, what-was-learned, or reusable-lesson
assistance. Passage assistance, if retained by a later consumer, is optional, local, human-facing,
non-authoritative, does not determine Story readiness, does not create an Insight, does
not require a lesson, and remains excluded from release unless separately authorized.

## Voice

Use writing that is concrete, specific, technically faithful, Evidence-backed, neutral, and
readable by humans and future Agents. Use standard terminology, explicit subjects, direct verbs,
and one main claim per sentence when practical. Preserve uncertainty and source limitations.

Supported surprise, disagreement, correction, failure, progress, and concrete results may provide
interest. Stylistic energy may organize and clarify Evidence; it may not embellish it.

Do not use metaphors, analogies, slogans, literary framing, colloquial filler, anthropomorphism,
vague AI boilerplate, abstract noun chains, or `X, not Y` contrast formulas and localized
equivalents. Reject phrases such as `The team needed to`, `This passage shows`, `This highlights the
importance of`, `The key takeaway is`, `The project learned`, `The evidence wanted`, and `The
workflow fought back`; rewrite with a supported subject and direct action.

If reviewed Evidence does not determine a cause, use the localized equivalent of `Cause not
determined.` Never fill the gap with a likely explanation.

English is canonical. Chinese is an optional localized sidecar. When present, it should be natural
and semantically equivalent, but missing or imperfect Chinese cannot block a validated English
Story. Never translate Evidence and present it as original.

## Narrative validation

For every Chapter, verify with safe structured data that:

- the Chapter is one complete coherent narrative arc;
- the complete approved history was considered before Chapter selection;
- chronology, continuity, attribution, causal restraint, uncertainty, Privacy, and Evidence support
  are preserved;
- supported ordinary context, progress, failures, corrections, consequences, and current state were
  not removed for lacking an Insight;
- the title and overview are supported without a tension/outcome proof requirement;
- People is nonempty and every actor/action attribution is supported;
- Story blocks use exact Chapter-contained Evidence and contain no fabricated or private source copy;
- Chapters were determined before adjacent Phase grouping;
- Phase labels are precise one- or two-word labels;
- Insights were selected only after the complete Story was understood;
- Insight cardinality is `0..n` with no quota;
- every existing Insight contains Background, Quote, Directly Acquired Experience, and Principle;
- every Quote uses safe same-Chapter Story anchors and every Insight has internal Evidence support;
- Insight title remains optional metadata;
- no Story block carries a mandatory lesson or passage-assistance obligation;
- standard terminology, neutral structure, failure retention, and non-fabrication remain intact.

Persist only the bounded source/readiness data defined by
[story-data-contract.md](story-data-contract.md). This proof is not model reasoning and must not
enter Workflow Progress or release output. A failed check keeps Build Project Story active.
